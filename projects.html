Generative peptide design with diffusion models

Feb. 2025 – Mar. 2026 · principal investigator

De novo peptide design is bottlenecked by a near-infinite sequence space, and most generated sequences never fold or bind. I extended the PepGLAD diffusion framework and built a much larger peptide–target complex training set to broaden its reach, then wired an external property predictor (solubility, stability) in as a constraint so generation is steered toward sequences likely to work as drugs — not just look plausible.

SYNBIP 2.0 — synthetic binding protein database

Jul. 2023 – Sept. 2024 · principal investigator

Synthetic binding proteins offer cheaper, easier-to-deliver alternatives to antibodies, yet the field's data was fragmented, inconsistently annotated, and missing the 3D-structure and epitope detail needed to design new ones. As lead of SYNBIP 2.0 I consolidated it: 3D structures for 899 SBP–target complexes, a fivefold expansion of the sequence space to 12,025 curated SBPs, and over 16 million candidate scaffolds mined from the PDB and AlphaFold DB — built from manual curation, protein–protein docking, structural alignment, AI annotation and automated web-mining.

Expanding the sequence space of synthetic binding proteins

Nov. 2022 – Mar. 2023 · principal investigator

Redesigning SBPs by site-directed mutagenesis is capped by its reliance on a known parent structure. I used ProteinMPNN to generate sequences from structure alone — 7,245 candidates across 55 scaffolds, screened in silico — and the designed proteins showed better solubility and thermal stability than known SBPs. Eight scaffolds stood out as especially amenable to deep-learning design, a practical map of where the method earns its keep.
