I trained as a computational biologist at the School of Pharmaceutical Sciences, Chongqing University, and now work as an AI Protein Computational Engineer at Shenzhen Liying Biotechnology — applying generative and structure-based methods to CNS drug discovery. My focus is synthetic binding proteins (SBPs) — small engineered binders that compete with antibodies on specificity and affinity while being cheaper to produce and easier to deliver.

My work runs along three threads. I maintain SYNBIP, the reference database for synthetic binding proteins, and co-led its 2.0 release. I apply deep-learning design frameworks such as ProteinMPNN to expand the sequence space of known SBP scaffolds. And most recently I have been training a diffusion-based generative model for peptide design, with an external property predictor wired in as a constraint.

What ties this together is a bias toward verification: solubility, thermal stability and binding affinity get checked rather than assumed, whether the candidate came out of a generative model or a docking run.
